# Client Kickoff Call — Split

**Project:** Split — Group Expense App  
**Project Type:** Short Motion Graphic  
**Duration:** 30–45 seconds  
**Primary Format:** 16:9  
**Secondary Format:** 9:16 adaptation  
**Participants:**  
- Maya — Client / Founder of Split
- Alex — Project Manager
- Designer

---

### 00:00 — Introduction

**Alex:**  
Hey Maya, thanks for joining. So we're working on the launch video for Split. Before we get into the creative side, can you tell us a little bit about the product and what you're hoping the video will achieve?

**Maya:**  
Yeah, absolutely. Split is basically an app for those situations where you're out with friends and nobody wants to deal with the bill afterward.

You know the situation. One person pays for dinner, someone else paid for the taxi, someone bought drinks, and then three days later you're all trying to figure out who owes who.

We wanted to make that whole process painless.

---

### 02:00 — The Product

**Alex:**  
So what does Split actually do?

**Maya:**  
You create a group in the app, add the expenses, and Split works out who owes what.

You can split things equally, or you can assign specific items to specific people.

So if four of us go for dinner and I only had a salad while someone else ordered the giant steak, we're not pretending everyone owes the same amount.

The app keeps track of everything and tells you exactly who needs to pay whom.

---

### 04:00 — Why Split Exists

**Alex:**  
What made you build it?

**Maya:**  
Honestly, we were tired of the awkwardness.

Nobody wants to be the person who says, “Hey, you still owe me $12.”

And nobody wants to be sitting around at the end of dinner doing maths while everyone else is ready to leave.

The money isn't usually the biggest problem. It's the friction around it.

---

### 06:00 — The Audience

**Alex:**  
Who are you mainly trying to reach with this launch?

**Maya:**  
Mostly young adults who regularly go out with friends.

People in their twenties and thirties. Students, young professionals, people living with roommates — basically anyone who regularly shares expenses with other people.

They already use apps for pretty much everything, so we don't need to explain what an app is.

The important thing is that they immediately recognize the situation we're talking about.

---

### 08:00 — The Problem

**Alex:**  
If someone watches the video and remembers only one thing, what should that be?

**Maya:**  
That splitting expenses with friends doesn't have to be awkward or complicated.

I don't want the video to feel like we're explaining accounting software.

It should feel like:

**“Oh my god, that's exactly what happens with my friends.”**

And then:

**“Wait, there's an app for that?”**

---

### 10:00 — The Current Experience

**Alex:**  
Can you describe what normally happens when a group gets a bill?

**Maya:**  
It's always some version of the same thing.

Someone grabs the bill.

Someone takes a photo of it.

Someone opens the calculator.

Then everyone starts remembering what they ordered.

Someone says, “I only had one drink.”

Someone else says, “Didn't you have some of my fries?”

Then there's usually Venmo or bank transfers afterward.

And somehow there's always one person who says, “I'll send it tomorrow.”

And they don't.

**Alex:**  
Sounds familiar.

**Maya:**  
Exactly. That's the feeling I want the video to capture.

---

### 12:00 — What Makes Split Different

**Alex:**  
There are already expense-splitting apps. Why should someone use Split?

**Maya:**  
We made it incredibly simple.

You shouldn't need to understand how the app works before using it.

The whole experience is built around one idea:

**Add what was spent. Split it. Done.**

We're also trying to make the product feel more social and less like a financial tool.

You're using it with your friends. It shouldn't feel like you're doing taxes.

---

### 14:00 — Tone

**Alex:**  
How should the video feel?

**Maya:**  
Fun.

Definitely not corporate.

A little witty would be great.

It should feel like something you'd actually want to watch on Instagram rather than an advertisement explaining five product features.

But I don't want it to become childish or overly goofy either.

The humor should come from situations people recognize.

---

### 16:00 — Visual Direction

**Alex:**  
Do you have a particular animation style in mind?

**Maya:**  
Not really.

We're open to the creative direction.

We've seen videos where everything is just screenshots of the app flying around the screen, and we don't want that.

The product needs to be clear, obviously, but I'd rather see an actual story where the app becomes part of the story.

---

### 18:00 — Brand

**Alex:**  
Do you already have a visual identity for Split that we need to follow?

**Maya:**  
Not really. Split is still pretty new, so we haven't locked everything down yet.

We're open to the creative direction.

We'd like the video to feel modern, social, and approachable, but we're happy for you to explore what that means visually.

We're especially open when it comes to color, typography, illustration, and the overall graphic style.

We don't want anything that feels too corporate or like a traditional finance product.

**Alex:**  
So the visual direction is open, but it still needs to feel appropriate for the audience and the personality of the product?

**Maya:**  
Exactly. We'd rather see a strong creative direction that makes sense for Split than have you follow a style just because it's trendy.
---

### 20:00 — Deliverables

**Alex:**  
What exactly do you need from the video?

**Maya:**  
The main version will be around 30 to 45 seconds.

We'll use it on our website and in presentations, so 16:9 is the main format.

We'll also use it on Instagram and TikTok, so we'd like the concept to work in a vertical version too.

And we'd like a voiceover.

---

### 22:00 — What the Video Needs to Communicate

**Alex:**  
Are there any specific features that absolutely need to be shown?

**Maya:**  
Not necessarily all of them.

The important thing is that people understand the basic idea.

You add the expenses, Split figures out who owes what, and everyone can settle up.

If there's a clever way to show the individual-item splitting, that would be really good, but I don't want to cram every feature into 40 seconds either.

---

### 24:00 — What to Avoid

**Alex:**  
Anything you definitely don't want?

**Maya:**  
Please don't make it feel like a banking commercial.

And no generic “happy friends looking at their phones” stock footage.

Also, don't make the whole thing a UI walkthrough.

We want people to remember the **idea**, not just what the interface looks like.

---

### 26:00 — Final Thought

**Alex:**  
If you could describe the feeling you want someone to have after watching it in one sentence, what would it be?

**Maya:**  
I want them to finish the video and immediately think of that one friend who still owes them money.

**Alex:**  
Perfect. I think that's enough for us to get started.

Thanks, Maya.

**Maya:**  
Thank you. Excited to see what you come up with.
